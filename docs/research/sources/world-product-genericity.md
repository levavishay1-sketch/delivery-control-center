## 1. How multi-organization work-management tools model organizations

**PSA common shape (across all of the above):** Client/Company/Account → Contract/Deal/Retainer (the *budget type* and *rates*) → Project → Task → Time entry → Invoice. **Internal work** is modelled as "a project whose client is ourselves" or as a non-billable budget — never as a separate entity type. The contract/budget layer is what DCC currently lacks between `client` and `project`.

### 1.6 Synthesis for §1 — three ways the world does "client sees only their part"

1. **Hard containers** (ADO organization/project, Jira site/project, ClickUp Space/Folder, Teamwork external company): simple, but everything shared across containers (repos, processes, people) becomes friction.
2. **Row-level scoping + guest identities** (monday shareable boards, Asana guests, ADO area-path permissions + Stakeholder license, Teamwork client users): the requester is *inside* the tool with a cheap/free licence and sees a filtered slice.
3. **A separate requester surface** (JSM portal, monday service customer portals, white-label client portals; Linear's customers never enter at all — the CRM is the surface): the requester sees *requests and status*, never the delivery board.

DCC's `client_id` + RLS is (2) at the data layer. Every mature product in this list also has (3). A delivery cockpit and a requester portal are different screens with different vocabulary; §7 covers the vocabulary.

## 3. Org shapes with a requirement → delivery lifecycle

For each shape: who asks, who approves (scope and acceptance), who builds, what the tracker is, what is special. The matrix at the end condenses this.

1. **Software house / agency (DCC's pilot).** Requester = a client contact (often non-technical); scope approver = client's product/business owner + agency PM; acceptor = client; developer = agency devs (and now agents); tracker = agency's ADO/Jira, sometimes the *client's* tracker; special = budget/retainer per contract, per-client stack, language and ways of working, rates and profitability (PSA §1.5), client must not see other clients. Evidence: §1.3–1.5.
2. **In-house IT serving business departments.** Requester = department (ITIL customer/user/sponsor split); approver = department head (sponsor) + IT demand/portfolio board (SAFe LPM); developer = internal dev team or outsourced; tracker = JSM/ServiceNow request → Jira/ADO work item; special = no invoice but chargeback/capacity, formal demand intake, two records per need (request + dev item). Evidence: §2.
3. **ISV / product company with customer-driven features.** Requester = many customers via support/sales (Linear Customer Requests: customer record with tier/revenue, request attached to issue or project); approver = product management (roadmap), never the customer; acceptor = internal (release); tracker = Linear/Jira + CRM; special = aggregation across customers, priority by revenue/tier, customers never see the board. Evidence: §1.2.
4. **Consultancy / SI running many engagements.** Requester = client sponsor; approver = client PO + engagement manager; acceptor = client per SOW milestones; developer = consultants, often on the client's systems; tracker = the client's (ADO/Jira) with a PSA on top for time and revenue; special = *engagement* (SOW, budget type, rates) is the primary entity, "limited visibility into non-billable work" is a known pain (Kantata). Wipro adopting Factory shows SIs want agent-native delivery across client engagements. Evidence: §1.5, §4.
5. **Freelancer / micro-studio.** Requester = the client; approver = the client; developer = the freelancer; tracker = a client portal (white-label portals with custom domain/branding/forms) or the client's tool; special = one person holds all provider roles; branding matters; invoicing per task. Evidence: §6 (portals) [C].
6. **Open-source maintainers.** Requester = any reporter (issue); triage = SIG/triager ("New issues are automatically assigned a `needs-triage` label"; `/triage accepted`; `priority/*`, `kind/*`); developer = self-assigned contributor ("If you think you can fix the issue, assign it to yourself"); approval = two-phase `/lgtm` (reviewers: "general code quality, correctness") and `/approve` (approvers in OWNERS files: "holistic acceptance criteria… compatibility, API and flag definitions"); merge = automation ("Prow automatically merges the PR"). Special = no client, no budget, ownership scoped per directory, 30-day stale rule. Evidence: [A] https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/master/contributors/guide/issue-triage.md ; https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/master/contributors/guide/owners.md
7. **Government contractor.** Requester = agency users via Product Owner; approver = PO (scope) and CO (contract); acceptor = COR against a QASP; developer = vendor team; tracker = vendor's or agency's; special = written delegation of authority, acceptance is a legal act, CMMI-style formal change control ("Requirement, Change Request, Risk, and Review" WITs). Evidence: §2 [A].
8. **Platform team serving internal teams.** Requester = stream-aligned teams; approver = platform PM; developer = platform team; tracker = internal backlog + self-service; special = intake should be self-service, "requester must have a firm say"; measured by adoption/cognitive load, not tickets closed. Evidence: §2 [B]/[C].
9. **Citizen-developer / Power Platform shop.** Requester = maker (business user) who *also* builds; approver = admin/CoE (environment request, DLP, risk tier); developer = maker + pro-dev for the hard parts (fusion); tracker = CoE Starter Kit / environment requests; special = governance by environment group and risk rating, "macro vs micro" policy levels, huge volume (18k environments). Evidence: §2 [A].
10. **MSP / managed-services provider (support + small dev).** Requester = client end users (tickets) and client managers (projects); approver = client contact per agreement; developer = MSP engineers; tracker = PSA (ConnectWise/Autotask: Company → Contact → Ticket/Project → Agreement) — *not fetched this session*, listed from prior knowledge [C]; special = agreements (SLA, hours bank) drive what is billable. Microsoft's multitenant guidance explicitly excludes the MSP-with-own-customers case ("This series isn't intended to provide guidance for those scenarios… likely to be helpful for ISVs who build software for MSPs") — i.e. two-level tenancy is a recognised, separate problem [A] https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/guide/multitenant/overview .
11. **Startup / single-product team.** Requester = founders/PM; approver = same; developer = team + agents; tracker = Linear; special = one tenant, "client" is the company itself; wants the opinionated default, zero configuration (Linear's philosophy §5).
12. **Enterprise with mixed vendor + internal delivery.** Requester = business unit; approver = internal PO; developer = internal team *plus* one or more vendors, each with its own tracker; special = the vendor is the "client" of nothing — DCC's roles invert (the enterprise is the operator, vendors are developer orgs with restricted views), and cross-tracker sync matters.

### 4.3 Where the gap is

- **Every control center above is bounded by one organization's identity and one tracker.** Governance is enterprise → org → repo (GitHub), workspace → team (Linear), tenant → environment group (Power Platform). The closest thing to "per-client policy" is GitHub's *organization custom properties* ("region, compliance tier, or department") — but that targets GitHub orgs, and the doc warns it is evaluated once.
- **Nobody models the requester side.** Agents take an *issue* as the prompt ("The issue you assign is effectively the prompt"); the path from a phone call to an accepted deliverable, with gaps, approvals and acceptance by an outside party, lives nowhere in these products. Linear's Customer Requests is the only first-class requester record, and it is deliberately internal-only.
- **The many-clients case is served by generic platforms plus the service firm's own process** (Wipro + Factory; Carahsoft public sector). That is proof of demand and absence of product at the same time.
- **Nearest primitives to reuse rather than rebuild**: GitHub agents/third-party agents + policy by custom property; Linear `AgentSession` states (`pending/active/awaitingInput/error/complete/stale`) as a vocabulary for DCC's session events; Cursor/Devin multi-repo environments and "Spaces" as the unit an agent sees.
- **Not found**: any product doing "one control center across many client repos with agents *and* requirement-to-acceptance orchestration". Caveat: search budget and blocked domains; a niche agency-focused product could exist unseen. The category names in 2026 are "software factory", "agent command center", "mission control", "agent HQ" — none says "client".

## 5. Configurability patterns and cautionary tales

| Pattern / tale | What the evidence says | Grade / source |
|---|---|---|
| **Jira's workflow engine — over-configuration** | "some tickets of type 'Task' containing more than 300 custom fields"; "teams add hundreds of custom fields, screens, workflows, dashboards, and projects, with few getting retired"; "the team serving Jira rather than Jira serving the team"; "the attempt to standardize Jira across an organization and force teams to adhere to processes that make management's life easier but the teams' life more difficult". | **Linear — opinionated "one way"** | "there's one really good way of doing things"; "narrowed the surface area of choice, with strong defaults, limited options, and the product gently but firmly nudging users toward a particular way of working"; default flow Triage → Backlog → In Progress. Never let a client define a workflow; let them pick a shape and a vocabulary.

## 6. Multi-tenant SaaS structure for agencies

### 6.1 Tenancy and the two-level problem

- Azure Architecture Center: define the tenant first — "Consider who your customer is or who receives your services"; in B2B "a single tenant typically has multiple users"; tenants ≠ customers 1:1 ("A single customer might want to maintain two instances of your service so that they can keep their development and production environments separated"). Models: automated single-tenant (silo), fully multitenant (pool), vertically partitioned (premium isolated tiers), horizontally partitioned (shared app, per-tenant DB). [A] https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/guide/multitenant/considerations/tenancy-models
- The MSP shape (a tenant that has its own customers) is *explicitly out of scope* of Microsoft's guidance — "This series isn't intended to provide guidance for those scenarios" — which confirms DCC is a **two-level tenancy**: operator (the software house) → tenant (its client) → users, and a **three-level** one if DCC is ever sold to several software houses. [A] https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/guide/multitenant/overview
- Multi-organization Azure DevOps is chosen for "Distinct customer data isolation requirements" and "Different billing and cost center requirements" [A] (§1.3) — DCC's per-client RLS gives isolation inside one deployment; some clients will still demand a silo.

### 6.2 Global vs per-client settings — how the best-known systems resolve them

| System | Resolution rule | Grade / source |
|---|---|---|
| **GitLab cascading settings** | Instance → group → subgroup → project. Effective value order: "Returns the dirty value, if the attribute has changed" → "Return locked ancestor value" → "Return locked instance-level application settings value" → "Return this namespace's attribute, if not nil" → "Return value from nearest ancestor where value is not nil" → "Return instance-level application setting". Settings "can be 'locked' (enforced) on groups lower in the hierarchy". | [A] https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/cascading_settings.html (fetched via https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gitlabhq/gitlabhq/master/doc/development/cascading_settings.md) |
| **GitHub enterprise policies** | Each policy is either enforced or delegated: "you can set a base repository permission level… or allow owners to administer the setting on the organization level"; "Each repository inherits a default force push setting from… the organization… Each organization… inherits… from the enterprise"; "Enforce on all repositories" overrides. | [A] https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/admin/enforcing-policies/enforcing-policies-for-your-enterprise/enforcing-repository-management-policies-in-your-enterprise |
| **GitHub Copilot agents** | Enterprise policy → selected orgs (by custom property) → org may block repos ("If there are repositories where Copilot cloud agent should be blocked for all users, tell organization owners to configure this setting"); agent profiles at enterprise/org/repo; MCP at repo or enterprise. | [A] §4.2 |
| **Azure DevOps processes** | System process (locked) → inherited process (bounded edits) → many projects share one process; "any updates Microsoft makes to system processes automatically update in inherited processes". | [A] §1.3 |
| **Power Platform** | Tenant policies (macro) → environment groups by department/BU/cost center with rules and DLP aligned per group → environment; makers request, admins create. | [A] §2 |

**Common pattern:** at every level a setting is a pair *(value, locked?)*; most-specific wins **unless an ancestor locked it**; templates seed the top. DCC's `global → client → work item` has the first half. Missing, per this evidence: (1) a **lock/enforce** bit on global and client values; (2) a **project** (or engagement/contract) level between client and work item — every PSA and ADO/GitLab hierarchy has it, and budgets/approval rules live there; (3) **shape templates** that seed a client's settings on creation.

### 6.3 Per-client branding, portals, policies

- White-label client portals (Copilot.app, SuiteDash, Clinked, Moxo, Agiled, ManyRequests…) all offer "custom domains, branded login pages, and emails from your agency domain"; Clinked adds "a branded mobile app published under your name"; the portal shows each client only its own items and hosts intake forms. [C] https://www.clinked.com/blog/9-best-white-label-client-portal-software-in-2026-honest-comparison ; https://agiled.app/blog/best-client-portal-software-for-agencies ; https://www.moxo.com/blog/best-white-label-client-portal
- monday service: "Multiple Customer Portals" per account [A] (§1.4). Teamwork: client users bound to an external company, free, capped at 5 active projects [A] (§1.5).
- Per-client policies seen in the wild: budget type and rates per contract (PSA); approval rules per request type (JSM) and per repo (branch policies); spend limits per organization (Claude Cowork "spend limits"; GitHub AI credits; Cursor admin allow/blocklists) [A]/[B] §4.2.
- **Cross-client shared assets**: GitHub keeps shared things at the *enterprise* level (custom agent profiles, MCP registries) while data stays in orgs; GitLab shares via group links; ADO shares *processes* across projects but warns that separate organizations cannot share anything. For DCC's "repo shared across clients", the evidence says: model the shared repo as an **operator-owned asset** with per-client *visibility of events on it*, not as a row with one `client_id` — otherwise it collides with "distinct customer data isolation".

### 7.3 Usability evidence on naming for non-technical users

## 8. Implications for DCC's data model (what recurs across all shapes)

Entities that appear in nearly every shape and are missing or implicit in `client → project → work item`:

1. **Operator** (the organisation running DCC — the software house, the IT department, the platform team). Today it is implicit ("DCC Internal" client). Two-level tenancy needs it explicit, and three-level if DCC is sold to other operators.
2. **Requester organisation** (client / department / customer / agency) — keep `client`, but it is a *type* with a shape (§3) and a vocabulary (§7).
3. **Engagement / contract / budget** between operator and requester org (PSA "deal/retainer/budget", SAFe "Lean business case", government "SOW + QASP", MSP "agreement"): budget type, rates, approval rules, SLA, spend limits for agents. This is where per-client policy lives in every PSA; settings resolution should have a level here (or `project` should carry it).
4. **Shared asset** (a repo, library, template) owned by the operator, visible per client by policy — not a row with one `client_id`.
5. **Roles on the requester side as separate people**: requester, user, sponsor (budget), scope approver, acceptor; on the operator side: relationship owner (BRM/account manager), developer, reviewer, agent-identity owner. The 18F and ITIL evidence says collapsing these into "the client" breaks the moment two people disagree.
6. **Settings as (value, locked?)** at each level, with shape templates seeding them.
7. **Two surfaces with two vocabularies**: requester portal (request/status/acceptance) and delivery cockpit (work item/session/checks) — every mature product has both.
8. **Agent sessions as a standard state machine** (`pending/active/awaitingInput/error/complete/stale`) so DCC's event log can interoperate with Linear/GitHub agent records rather than invent a fourth vocabulary.
